Task Description

The combined effects of space environmental stressors induce pathologies in multiple organ systems. Specifically, losses to the musculoskeletal system may be very dangerous for the health and performance of astronauts on extended duration missions on the Moon or arriving on Mars. A critical need remains to better understand the impact of radiation exposure, one of the key environmental stressors of deep space, on human and animal physiology to enable extended duration missions beyond low Earth orbit or setting up settlements on the Moon or Mars. There is a critical gap in knowledge surrounding the impact of the space radiation environment on skeletal health and on the progress of fetal skeletal development during pregnancy. The objective of the current proposal is to capitalize on a unique tissue-sharing opportunity to examine the combined effects of continuous radiation exposure and pregnancy on maternal and fetal skeletal physiology. We propose to assess maternal and fetal skeletal physiology through measurements of mineral and material properties, as well as assess changes in cellular dynamics of the maternal bone under the microscope. This study will be critical in assessing how a very harmful component of the space radiation environment impacts multiple aspects of skeletal health, including sex-specific differences and individual variation in the impact of the space environment on the functioning of the body.

Task Progress Report

During the past reporting period, the assessment of fetal sample external morphology (AIM 2) continued, dissecting microscope images obtained, and microCT scanning (E18 samples only) began. Processing of the microCT scans has just begun. We continued the search for a research lab or company to perform the sex determination genetic testing of the samples and were finally able to locate a reputable commercial lab. We purposefully delayed all types of destructive analysis until all possible non-destructive analyses were complete. All available fetal data was presented at local conferences and the NASA Human Research Program (HRP) Investigators' Workshop (IWS). MicroCT scanning of the maternal tibia (AIM 1) has been completed at the proximal metaphyseal area and midshaft. Our team is actively working on analyses of the scans. All tibia and femur samples were shipped to the collaborator. 3-point-bend to failure mechanical testing has been completed and microindentation testing is underway. Plastic and paraffin embedding of the samples and the re-embedding is complete. Training of a new student is underway to complete the histologic sectioning; this process is slow as the samples are very rare. Issues with the computer attached to the microscope has delayed training for anaysis of all histologic sections.
