Task Description

Microgravity and Ground-based experimental studies of premixed-flame propagation in low-intensity turbulent flow under microgravity conditions are to be undertaken The focus of the research is on the hydrodynamic coupling between flame and flow-field dynamics at low turbulence intensities, which may lead to flame-generated turbulence, flame acceleration and enhancement of burning rates. As a result of hydrodynamic coupling the transition from initially planar or wrinkled laminar flames to fast turbulent flames or detonations is possible, even when diffusive-thermal effects associated with non-unity reactant Lewis numbers are not destabilizing. Therefore, the proposed work is relevant to microgravity fire safety.

Task Progress Report

During the past year, we have completed construction of a new non-rotating burner to be used for ground-based and microgravity studies of the Darrieus-Landau hydrodynamic instability on annular-flame propagation. We have also developed and tested new optical tools to enable measurements of turbulence properties in a second, rotating burner to be under construction soon. Preliminary ground-based studies will be carried out at UC Davis over the next few months, after-which microgravity experiments at the NASA Glenn Research Center will be conducted.
