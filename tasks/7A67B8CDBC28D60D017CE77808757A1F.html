Task Description

The primary objective of this research is to quantify how buoyancy affects flow instability and structure in the near field of low-density jets. In particular, we are interested in studying low-density jets that display self-excited behavior on Earth. By quantifying the flow field in both normal and reduced gravity, we plan to understand the fundamental basis for this instability. This research has three scientific objectives: (1) investigate the effects of gravity on flow instability and structure of low-density, buoyant jets (in normal gravity) that display self-excited behavior. By removing buoyancy in our experiments, we seek to obtain direct physical evidence of the instability mechanism. (2) determine if the instability in momentum-driven, low-density jets is buoyancy driven. By eliminating buoyancy in microgravity environment, globally as well as locally, we seek to examine our hypothesis that the instability mechanism in self-excited momentum-driven driven jets is buoyancy driven. (3) perform linear stability analysis of low-density gas jets with the inclusion of gravitational effects. We seek to study the characteristics of both convective and absolute instabilities in low-density jets and demarcate the boundary separating the two instability modes. Low-density jets occur in exhaust from engines and stacks in the industry, geophysical events such as volcanic eruptions, fuel leaks, diffusion flames, and accidental fires. The jet flows are also important in extraterrestrial applications, for example, in space propulsion, materials processing in space and other planets. The fluid dynamic instabilities in the shear layer between the jet and ambient fluid, the formation and characteristics of large-scale coherent structures, entrainment of the ambient fluid into the jet flow, and the mean flow field, which affect the evolution and transport of the structures are important near-field phenomena. The near-field jet behavior has direct influence on the acoustic noise and flow development in the far-field. The study closes major gaps in understanding of how buoyancy affects the flow instability and transition in low-density jets. The study will determine if buoyancy effects on the flow instability can be characterized using global parameters.
